# AMANAH CASH — DESIGN BRIEF v2.0

## 0. Purpose

This document replaces all prior explorations, handoffs, and design directions.
It is the single source of truth for the Amanah Cash monogram.

Every future concept, sketch, and production asset must satisfy every clause in this document.
No clause is optional. No clause may be traded against another.

---

## 1. Brand Essence

**Amanah Cash** is a financial stewardship platform.

The word *amanah* (أمانة) means **trustworthiness** — the quality of being worthy of confidence. A person who carries amanah is a custodian: they safeguard what has been entrusted to them and return it faithfully.

The logo must communicate:
- Custodianship — something is being safeguarded
- Verification — something has been checked and confirmed
- Illumination — clarity, transparency, nothing hidden
- Permanence — this trust is structural, not decorative

The logo must NOT communicate:
- Speed, motion, energy, sport
- Cryptocurrency, Web3, blockchain
- Generic technology, startup culture
- Decoration, ornament, flourish

## 2. The Six Immutable Requirements

These requirements are frozen. They were approved through extensive exploration and may not be reinterpreted, relaxed, or traded.

### R1 — The Letter A Is Discovered, Never Announced

The mark must NOT read as a letter A on first impression.

The first impression must be: **a unique geometric symbol.**

The second impression (within 1-3 seconds) must be: **"This is also the letter A."**

This means:
- The mark may NOT start from a typed, drawn, or typographic capital A.
- The mark MUST contain sufficient letter DNA (two converging masses, a horizontal structural element, an apex or peak) that the A-reading emerges naturally from the geometry.
- If a viewer is told "there is an A in this logo" and cannot find it within 5 seconds, the mark fails.

### R2 — The Checkmark Is Discovered, Never Announced

The mark must NOT contain a visible checkmark symbol.

The checkmark reading must emerge from the **direction** of the light passage (R3) and the **asymmetry** of the geometry (R5).

This means:
- No checkmark shape may be drawn, pasted, overlaid, or attached.
- The verification gesture must be implied by a directional element — a beam, seam, or passage — that ascends from lower-left to upper-right.
- The discovery must feel inevitable: once seen, the viewer cannot unsee it.

### R3 — Light Is the Identity

The mark's defining feature is a **passage of light** through solid geometry.

The light is NOT:
- a glow effect
- a gradient
- a decorative cut
- a separate layer or accent

The light IS:
- a **structural seam** — a narrow passage through the form where light enters on one side and exits on the other
- the **only internal event** in an otherwise solid object
- the **memory hook** — the one feature that makes this mark unmistakable

The seam must feel as though the entire form was constructed around it. The form exists because the light needed a passage. The light has meaning because the form gives it structure.

### R4 — One Structural Object

The mark must be perceived as **one object** — a single mass, a single form, a single silhouette.

The mark may NOT be:
- an assembly of separate shapes
- a letter with an accent attached
- two or more elements placed in proximity
- a composition of layered parts

The mark MUST be:
- one continuous geometric construction
- inseparable into components without destroying the whole
- carved from one conceptual piece — as if CNC-machined from a single billet

If a viewer can point to any sub-element and say "that piece was added," the mark fails.

### R5 — Controlled Asymmetry

The mark must be **fundamentally balanced** — grounded, stable, trustworthy.

Asymmetry is permitted ONLY through the light passage (R3). The seam's diagonal direction is the sole source of visual asymmetry. This asymmetry creates:
- the checkmark reading (R2) — the seam ascends from lower-left to upper-right
- the forward-motion gesture — the light moves in a specific direction
- the perceptual trigger — the eye notices the diagonal passage against the stable form

Every other element — proportions, masses, corner radii, edge weights — must be symmetrical or balanced. If the seam were removed, the remaining form must be perfectly at rest.

### R6 — Scale Independence

The mark must function at:

| Scale | Context | Minimum Requirement |
|-------|---------|-------------------|
| **16px** | Browser favicon | The silhouette is recognizable and distinct from a generic shape. The light passage may simplify to a single-pixel directional feature but must not vanish entirely. |
| **24px** | Navigation bar | The A-reading begins to emerge. The seam is visible as a directional line. |
| **32px** | App icon | Both the A-reading and the checkmark direction are perceptible. |
| **48px** | Login mark | Full dual-read: A first, checkmark second, light throughout. |
| **512px+** | Master asset | All details resolve with industrial precision. |

At EVERY scale, the mark must look intentional — never accidental, never broken, never generic.

## 3. Visual Language

### Material Metaphor

The mark should feel as though it was **manufactured** — not illustrated.

Reference disciplines:
- **Industrial design** (Leica, Braun, Bang & Olufsen) — precision, materiality, restraint
- **Architecture** (Tadao Ando, Peter Zumthor) — light as structural medium, mass as presence
- **Type design** (optical correction, stroke modulation, counter balance) — geometry tuned for the human eye, not the coordinate system

The mark should feel like a **solid object that was precision-engineered to let light pass through.** Not a graphic. Not an illustration. An object.

### Perceptual Mechanics

The mark must create a **two-stage perception**:

**Stage 1 (0-500ms):** The eye registers a solid geometric form with a distinctive light passage. The impression is: "a substantial, engineered object with a precise opening for light." The viewer feels weight, precision, and illumination.

**Stage 2 (1-5 seconds):** The eye discovers structure within the form. The converging masses resolve into the letter A. The light passage's direction resolves into a verification checkmark. The viewer experiences recognition: "This is Amanah — trust, verified, illuminated."

Stage 1 must be instant and confident. Stage 2 must be rewarding but not difficult. The discovery should feel like recognition, not puzzle-solving.

## 4. Design Constraints

### What the Mark Must Have

- [ ] **Converging geometry** — two masses or edges that rise toward a peak or narrow top, creating the A-reading through proportion and direction (R1)
- [ ] **A horizontal structural element** — positioned at approximately 40-55% of the form's height, creating the A's crossbar reading (R1)
- [ ] **A diagonal light passage** — entering one side of the form, crossing the interior, and exiting the other side; ascending from lower-left to upper-right; creating the checkmark direction (R2, R3)
- [ ] **A unified silhouette** — one continuous outer boundary that is memorable even if all internal detail disappears (R4, R6)
- [ ] **Controlled corner radii** — all corners softened with consistent fillets that read as manufactured, not polygonal
- [ ] **Manufacturing discipline** — proportions that could be CNC-machined, embroidered, or cast in metal without losing integrity

### What the Mark Must NOT Have

- [ ] No typographic letter A — the A must emerge from geometry, not from typography
- [ ] No drawn checkmark — the check must emerge from direction, not from shape
- [ ] No gradients, glows, shadows, or effects — geometry only
- [ ] No separate layers or stacked elements — one object only
- [ ] No decorative cuts or ornamental features — every element must be structural
- [ ] No downward-pointing gestures — the light ascends, never descends
- [ ] No resemblance to traffic signs, clothing labels, Web3 emblems, or gaming iconography
- [ ] No flat-bottom triangular forms that read as yield signs or pendants

## 5. Silhouette Test (Mandatory)

Before any concept is accepted, it must pass ALL of these tests:

### Test A — Solid Fill
Fill the mark completely solid (no internal detail). The outer boundary must be:
1. Immediately recognizable as a unique shape — not a generic polygon
2. Memorable after one second of viewing
3. Distinct from common categories (tech, traffic, fashion, government)

### Test B — Seam Removal
Remove the light passage entirely. The remaining solid form must:
1. Still read as a deliberate, balanced object
2. Still contain enough geometry to suggest the letter A
3. Not feel broken or incomplete

### Test C — Blur
Apply a heavy Gaussian blur. The blurred mark must:
1. Retain one distinctive silhouette feature
2. Not collapse into a circle, square, or generic blob

### Test D — Polo Shirt
Imagine the mark embroidered in white thread on a black polo shirt. A stranger seeing it from two meters away must:
1. Notice the mark (it has presence)
2. Perceive it as a logo (it reads as intentional branding)
3. Feel curious enough to ask "what is that?" (it has intrigue)

### Test E — Category Test
Place the mark next to Apple, Notion, Linear, Stripe, and Vercel. It must:
1. Hold its own as a peer — not look weaker, simpler, or more generic
2. Not resemble any of them specifically
3. Read as a custom monogram — not a typed letter, not a generic icon

## 6. Lessons Inherited from Prior Explorations

These lessons are permanent. They constrain all future work.

### From the A+Check Assembly Phase
- **Lesson:** Assembling an A and a checkmark as separate elements always produces a two-object perception. The mark must be one object where both readings emerge from the same geometry.

### From the Industrial Monolith Phase
- **Lesson:** Manufacturing discipline (consistent radii, proportional precision, material-weight proportions) elevates a geometric mark from "computer-generated polygon" to "engineered object." This discipline must be preserved.
- **Lesson:** "Weighted stillness" — the feeling that a heavy mass is perfectly at rest — is the correct emotional target for a financial stewardship brand. The mark should feel grounded and permanent.
- **Lesson:** The silhouette must be memorable independently of internal detail. If the mark's identity depends solely on an internal feature that disappears at small sizes, the mark fails at favicon scale.

### From the Light Seam Phase
- **Lesson:** A diagonal passage of light through a solid form is the correct metaphor for the brand. Light = transparency, illumination, verification. Solid form = trust, custodianship, permanence. The passage of light through structure IS the brand.
- **Lesson:** The seam must be structural — the form was born with it, not cut afterward. The seam must enter and exit the geometry. It must never terminate inside.
- **Lesson:** The seam must be narrower than every structural stroke. The structure dominates; the light reveals.

### From the Failed Audit
- **Lesson:** Design drift is the most dangerous failure mode. Each "refinement" must be measured against the original requirements. If a requirement is no longer satisfied, the direction is wrong — no matter how sophisticated the execution.
- **Lesson:** Philosophical justifications (equilibrium, composure, prediction mismatch) must serve the concept, not replace it. If the philosophy and the geometry diverge, the geometry is wrong.
- **Lesson:** The etymology of "amanah" means trustworthiness and custodianship — not equilibrium or zero-sum reconciliation. The mark must express trust, not balance sheets.

## 7. Production Requirements

### Vector Format
- Single SVG file using `currentColor` for theme inheritance
- `fill-rule="evenodd"` where cutouts are needed
- No embedded raster data, no filters, no external references
- ViewBox must be square (e.g., `0 0 512 512`) with the mark optically centered

### 16px Optimization
- A dedicated `mark-16.svg` may be created with pixel-snapped coordinates
- At 16px, the light passage must render as at least 1px of directional feature
- The silhouette must remain distinct from a plain polygon

### Physical Media
- The mark must be expressible as:
  - Embroidered thread on fabric (the seam as a gap between stitched zones)
  - CNC-milled aluminum (the seam as a precision channel)
  - Blind-debossed on paper (the seam as an indented line)
  - Cast metal badge (the seam as a through-cut)

### Color
- Primary: teal (`#14A89B` light mode, `#38D0BF` dark mode) — the brand color
- The mark must also function in pure black and pure white without losing identity

## 8. Acceptance Criteria

A concept is accepted ONLY when ALL of the following are true:

1. [ ] The mark reads as one solid object with a passage of light
2. [ ] The A is discoverable within 5 seconds of being told it exists
3. [ ] The checkmark direction is discoverable within 5 seconds of being told it exists
4. [ ] The first impression is "unique symbol," not "letter A"
5. [ ] The silhouette passes the Solid Fill Test (Test A)
6. [ ] The mark passes the Blur Test (Test C)
7. [ ] The mark passes the Polo Shirt Test (Test D)
8. [ ] The mark holds its own next to Apple, Notion, Linear, Stripe, Vercel (Test E)
9. [ ] The mark is recognizable at 16px
10. [ ] The mark contains no gradients, glows, shadows, or effects
11. [ ] No sub-element can be identified as "added" or "attached"
12. [ ] The mark does not resemble traffic signs, Web3 emblems, or gaming iconography
13. [ ] Every corner has a consistent fillet radius
14. [ ] The light passage enters and exits the geometry — never terminates inside
15. [ ] The light passage ascends from lower-left to upper-right — never descends

## 9. Prohibited Directions

The following approaches have been explored and rejected. They may NOT be revisited:

| Direction | Reason for Permanent Rejection |
|-----------|-------------------------------|
| A typed letter A with a checkmark attached | Produces two-object perception (R4 violation) |
| A triangle with a crossbar pasted inside | Reads as typographic, not symbolic (R1 violation) |
| A pentagon, diamond, or shield as primary form | Loses letter DNA; becomes generic icon |
| A trapezoid with a downward chevron cutout | Abandons A-reading, checkmark, and light concept entirely |
| Organic curves or swooshes | Violates geometric precision requirement |
| Multiple geometric components assembled together | Violates one-object requirement (R4) |
| Decorative cuts or ornamental features | Violates structural-only requirement |
| Philosophy-first design (equilibrium, composure) that doesn't serve the concept | Produces marks that solve the wrong problem |

## 10. Governance

This document is governed by the acceptance criteria in Section 8.

No concept may proceed to production without passing every criterion.

The brief serves the brand. Not the designer. Not the philosophy. Not the process.
